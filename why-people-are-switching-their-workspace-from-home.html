Why People Are Switching Their Workspace from Home

Why people are switching, who is moving, and how to choose a coworking space.

You open your laptop on the dining table. Five minutes into a client call, the pressure cooker whistles. Then the power goes off, and your Wi-Fi goes with it.

If this sounds like your workday, you're not alone. Working from home felt easy at first. No traffic, no dress code, tea whenever you want. But for many people, home has slowly become a hard place to get real work done.

That's why more people are choosing coworking spaces. A coworking space is a shared office where you pay for a desk by the day or the month. The Wi-Fi, power, furniture and meeting rooms come with it.

Below, you'll see why people are switching, who is moving, and how to choose a space. If you're weighing coworking vs. working from home, this will help you decide.

What Makes Working from Home So Hard?

The four biggest problems are distractions, no clear end to the workday, loneliness and a setup that was never meant for work.

Constant distractions at home

At home, someone always needs you. A relative drops by. The delivery boy calls. The TV is on in the next room, and the kids have online class at full volume.

Each small break feels harmless. But getting back into deep focus takes time, and a few breaks an hour can eat your whole morning.

Work never really ends.

When your bedroom is also your office, work follows you everywhere. You check one email at 11 PM, then reply to three more.

A lot of freelancers know this habit: they "just finish one more task" every night. Without a door to walk out of, the workday has no finish line.

Working alone every day

At home, there's no one to ask a quick question or share a small win with. Some people enjoy the quiet at first. After a few months, many start to feel cut off.

A home setup that isn't built for work

A dining chair is fine for dinner, not for eight hours of typing. Back pain and neck pain creep in.

Home Wi-Fi slows down when the whole family is streaming. And during the Kerala monsoon, a power cut can end your workday without warning.

Why Are People Moving to Coworking Spaces?

People move to coworking spaces because they get a quiet, ready-made office without the cost of renting one. Here are the main benefits of a coworking space.

It's easier to focus.

A coworking space is set up for one thing: work. When everyone around you is busy, it's easier to get busy too. There's no bed calling you for a quick nap.

A proper place to meet clients

Meeting a client in a noisy café or in your living room doesn't look very professional. A coworking space gives you a clean desk and a meeting room where you can talk properly.

Work stays at work.

When you leave the space, your workday ends. Home becomes a place to relax again.

You meet new people.

In a coworking space, the person at the next desk might be a designer, a developer or a small business owner. One Takees member wrote that they met people from different career backgrounds and were even invited to an iftar party.

Reliable Wi-Fi and power backup

You don't need to worry about slow internet or power cuts. Good spaces take care of both, so a monsoon storm doesn't ruin your deadline.

Flexible plans

There's no yearly contract. Take a day pass for one day or a monthly desk for a regular seat.

Cheaper than renting an office

Your own office means a deposit, furniture, internet and electricity bills. In a coworking space, one fee covers it all.

A business address

Some coworking spaces let you use their address for your business, GST registration and letters. This helps if you don't want to use your home address.

Who Is Switching to Coworking?

Anyone who works on a laptop can use one. These four groups often make the switch.

  • Freelancers and creators. Designers, writers and video editors need quiet hours, fast uploads and a place to meet clients. A coworking space for freelancers gives them all three.
  • Remote and hybrid employees. Some people in Kannur work for companies in Bengaluru, Kochi or the Gulf. They need an office-like place close to home.
  • Startups and small teams. A team of two to five people may not be ready to rent a full office. Shared desks or a small cabin let them grow step by step.
  • Students and exam aspirants. Students preparing for PSC, bank or other exams need long, quiet hours. A desk away from home helps them follow a study routine.

When Working from Home Still Makes Sense

Coworking isn't right for everyone. If you have a quiet room, a good chair, steady internet and enjoy working alone, home can work well for you.

Coworking also has costs. There's a membership fee, travel time to the space, and sometimes a little noise from others.

Many people choose a middle path. They work from home on quiet days and book a day pass when they need focus or have a client meeting.

How to Choose the Right Coworking Space in Kannur

Check these five things before you join.

  1. Location and commute. Pick a place close to home or on your daily route. Ask about parking for your bike or car.
  2. Internet and power. Ask to test the Wi-Fi speed. Check what happens during a power cut.
  3. Pricing plans. Compare day passes, monthly desks and private cabins. Ask what's included, like tea, coffee or printing.
  4. Atmosphere. Some spaces are quiet; others are lively. Look at how clean it is and how helpful the staff are.
  5. Try before you join. Book a single day first. One full workday will tell you more than any photo.
Is coworking right for you? A quick check:
  • Do you get distracted at home more than once an hour?
  • Do you often work late because your day has no clear end?
  • Do you feel lonely working alone?
  • Does your Wi-Fi or power often let you down?
  • Do you need a proper place to meet clients?
If you said yes to two or more, a coworking space is worth trying.
